It is one of the first questions small business owners ask us. A Facebook page is free, your customers are already on it, and it takes ten minutes to set up. So why pay for a website? The honest answer: a Facebook page is a great place to talk to customers, but it is a weak place to be found and a risky place to keep your whole business.

You do not own your Facebook page

Your page lives on Meta's platform, under Meta's rules. If your account is hacked, flagged by mistake or locked, your page, your photos and your messages can disappear overnight, and getting them back can take weeks. Changes to how Facebook shows posts can also cut how many of your followers actually see what you post, with nothing you can do about it.

A website on your own domain, such as yourbusiness.co.za, belongs to you. You decide what is on it and it stays up as long as you keep it paid for.

Many customers search on Google, not Facebook

When someone needs a plumber, a caterer or a laptop repair, they usually type it into Google: "caterer Pretoria East", "laptop repair near me". Google mostly shows websites and Google Business Profiles in those results. If you only have a Facebook page, you are easy to miss at the exact moment someone is ready to pay.

A website makes you look established

Customers check you out before they call. A clear website with your services, prices or price ranges, photos of your work, reviews and a proper email address like info@yourbusiness.co.za tells them you are a real, serious business. It matters even more when you quote for corporate clients or tenders, who often expect a website.

It works while you are busy

A website can answer the same questions you answer in Messenger every day: hours, prices, location, what is included. It can also take bookings, quote requests or orders at 11 at night, without you having to reply to each message first.

When a Facebook page is enough (for now)

If you are just testing an idea, all your customers come from word of mouth, or you sell mostly to people who already follow you, a well-run Facebook page can carry you for a while. Pair it with a free Google Business Profile so you also show up on Google Maps.

The best answer: use both

The businesses that do best online use each tool for what it is good at:

  • Facebook and Instagram to post regularly, show new work and chat with customers.
  • A website as your home base: found on Google, owned by you, open 24/7.
  • A Google Business Profile linking to your website, so you show up on Maps with reviews.

Every social media post can then point people back to your website, where they can book, buy or ask for a quote.

You do not need a big website to start. A simple one-page site with your services, contact details and a WhatsApp button already does most of the work, and it can grow with the business.
