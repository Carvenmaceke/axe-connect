South Africans know the routine: the lights go off, the Wi-Fi drops and the unsaved document is gone. But the bigger cost is often hidden. The surge when power comes back can quietly damage chargers, routers, computer power supplies and hard drives. A few simple precautions protect your equipment and keep you working.

Why power cuts damage electronics

When power is restored, the voltage can spike or fluctuate for a moment. Repeated spikes stress the electronics in chargers, power supplies and routers. Sudden shutdowns can also corrupt files if a computer was writing to the disk when power was lost. Over months, this shows up as chargers that stop working, routers that keep restarting and computers that will not boot.

1. Use surge protection

A surge protector plug or multi-plug absorbs spikes before they reach your devices. Look for one that clearly states surge protection (not just a plain extension lead), with a rating suited to your equipment. Plug your computer, monitor, router and fibre box into surge-protected sockets.

Surge protectors wear out after absorbing big spikes. If yours has a "protected" indicator light that has gone off, replace it.

2. Unplug sensitive equipment before scheduled outages

If you know when the power will go off, switch off and unplug desktop computers, printers and TVs beforehand, and plug them back in a few minutes after power returns, once it has stabilised. Laptops can stay on battery.

3. Keep the internet on with a router UPS

A laptop on battery is not much use without Wi-Fi. A router UPS (often called a DC UPS or mini UPS) is a small battery pack that powers the fibre box and router directly. Depending on the model and your devices, it can keep the internet running for several hours.

  • Check which output voltage your router and fibre box need (it is printed on their power adapters, often 12V).
  • Make sure the UPS has enough outputs, or the right cables, for both devices.
  • Test it by switching off the wall plug before the next outage.

4. A UPS for desktop computers

Desktop computers have no battery, so a power cut shuts them down instantly. A standard UPS gives you a few minutes to save your work and shut down properly. It is designed to bridge short gaps, not to run a computer for hours. For longer runtime across the office, an inverter and battery system installed by a qualified electrician is the right solution.

5. Save often and use autosave

  • Store working documents in OneDrive or Google Drive, so autosave keeps a copy as you work.
  • In Microsoft Office, switch on AutoSave (top left) for files stored in OneDrive.
  • Press Ctrl+S (or Cmd+S) out of habit, especially when you know an outage is coming.

6. Look after laptop batteries

Frequent outages mean laptops run on battery more often. Modern lithium batteries last longest when they are not constantly at 0% or 100%.

  • Avoid running the battery completely flat regularly.
  • Many laptops have a battery-care setting that limits charging to about 80% when you are usually plugged in. Use it.
  • Keep the laptop cool. Heat wears batteries out faster.

If your laptop has stopped charging, see our guide on checks before buying a new charger or battery.

7. Shut down properly after an outage

If a desktop computer lost power suddenly, let it start up normally and allow any disk checks to finish. If it starts showing errors, freezing or refusing to boot after outages, back up your files as soon as possible and get the drive checked. Early warning signs often come before total failure.

8. Back up regularly

Even with good protection, hardware fails. A cloud backup plus an external drive means a damaged computer does not mean lost work. Our guide to backing up your business files explains a simple routine.

Common questions

Can a power surge damage my laptop if it is charging?

It can damage the charger, and occasionally the laptop's charging circuit. A surge-protected plug reduces the risk. If your charger stopped working after a power cut, try a known-good charger of the correct type before assuming the laptop is faulty.

Is a power bank enough to run my router?

Some routers can run from a power bank with the right cable and voltage converter, but many need 12V, which normal USB power banks do not supply. A purpose-made router UPS is simpler and more reliable.

How long will a UPS keep my computer running?

A small desktop UPS usually gives a few minutes, enough to save and shut down. Runtime depends on the UPS's battery and how much power your equipment uses. Check the manufacturer's runtime chart for your load.
