Getting paid should be the easiest part of your day. Yet many small businesses still chase deposits on WhatsApp, wait for proof of payment and check the bank app every hour. Taking payments online removes most of that work and cuts down on no-shows. Here are the main options in South Africa and how to choose.

Option 1: Ordinary EFT with your bank details

The simplest option: you share your banking details and the customer pays by EFT. It costs you nothing extra and everyone knows how it works.

The downsides are real, though:

  • It is slow. Payments between different banks can take a day or more to clear.
  • It needs manual checking. You match each payment to a customer by reference, which is easy to get wrong when people use the wrong reference.
  • Fake proof of payment is common. A PDF that says "payment successful" proves nothing. Only money showing as available in your account counts.
Never release goods or confirm a booking based on a screenshot or PDF alone. Check that the money has cleared in your own banking app first.

Option 2: Payment links

A payment link is a web address you send to a customer by WhatsApp, SMS or email. They tap it, pay by card and you get a notification. Most South African card providers, including the ones that supply small card machines, offer payment links in their apps.

Payment links are a good next step if you mostly sell through WhatsApp or Instagram. There is nothing to build, and you see straight away who has paid.

Option 3: A payment gateway on your website

A payment gateway is the service that takes card or instant EFT payments on your website's checkout. In South Africa, well-known options include PayFast, Yoco, Ozow (instant EFT) and Peach Payments. The customer pays on a secure page run by the gateway, so your website never stores card numbers.

A gateway on your website makes sense when:

  • you sell products online and want orders to be paid before you pack them;
  • you take bookings and want a deposit before the slot is confirmed;
  • you want the payment linked to the order or booking automatically, without matching references by hand.

Card, instant EFT and QR payments

Most gateways let customers choose how to pay:

  • Card (credit or debit), the option most customers expect.
  • Instant EFT, where the customer logs in to their own bank and the payment is confirmed immediately. Useful for customers without cards or with low card limits.
  • QR payments through banking and payment apps, handy in a shop or at a market stall.

What does it cost?

Gateways and card providers charge a fee per transaction, usually a percentage of the amount, sometimes with a small fixed fee. Some also have a monthly fee. Prices change and differ between providers and plans, so compare current fees on each provider's website before you sign up.

When you compare, look at more than the percentage:

  • Payout time: how quickly the money reaches your bank account.
  • Monthly or setup fees, which matter more if you sell only a few times a month.
  • Refunds: how easy they are, and whether you are charged for them.
  • Verification: what documents they need from you, and how long approval takes.

Deposits: the cure for no-shows

For salons, photographers, consultants, tutors and anyone who works by appointment, a deposit at the time of booking changes everything. People who have paid show up, and when they do not, you are not left out of pocket.

A booking system with deposits works like this:

  1. The customer picks a service and an open time on your website.
  2. They pay a deposit, for example 50%, through the gateway.
  3. The booking is confirmed automatically and both of you get an email.
  4. If the deposit is not paid within a set time, the slot is released for someone else.

Make your deposit and cancellation rules clear on the booking page, for example how much notice is needed to reschedule and whether the deposit is refundable.

Keep your customers' information safe

  • Use a recognised gateway, so card details are entered on their secure page, never on your own forms.
  • Never ask customers to send card numbers by WhatsApp or email.
  • Collect only the personal information you need. South Africa's Protection of Personal Information Act (POPIA) expects you to protect what you collect.
  • Make sure your website has a padlock (HTTPS) on every page.

Which option suits you?

  • Selling mainly on WhatsApp or social media: start with payment links.
  • Taking appointments: online bookings with a deposit through a gateway.
  • Selling products: an online shop with a gateway at checkout, so only paid orders reach you.
  • Large business-to-business invoices: EFT is still normal, but send invoices with clear references and check payments in your bank, not on paper.

Common questions

Do I need a registered company to accept card payments?

Not always. Many providers accept sole proprietors as well as registered companies, but each has its own verification requirements, such as ID, proof of address and a bank account in the business's or owner's name. Check the provider's requirements before you start.

Can customers still pay by EFT if I add online payments?

Yes. Many businesses offer both: instant payment online for convenience, and EFT for customers who prefer it. The booking or order simply stays "awaiting payment" until you confirm the EFT has cleared.

Is it safe to take payments on a small business website?

It is, when the payment happens on the gateway's secure page and your site uses HTTPS. Your website only receives a message saying whether the payment succeeded, never the card details.
