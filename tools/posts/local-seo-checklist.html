Search for "plumber near me" or "laptop repairs Pretoria" and Google shows a map with three businesses, then a list of websites. Those top spots get most of the calls. Local SEO is the work that helps your business earn them. None of it is magic, and most of it you can do yourself.

How Google picks local results

Google explains that local results are mainly based on three things:

  • Relevance: how well your business matches what the person searched for.
  • Distance: how far you are from the searcher, or from the area they mentioned.
  • Prominence: how well known and trusted your business is, based on reviews, links, mentions and your website.

You cannot move closer to every customer, but you can improve relevance and prominence a lot.

1. Complete your Google Business Profile

This is the single most important step for the map results. Fill in every section: categories, services, service areas, hours, phone number, website and photos. Our guide on setting up a Google Business Profile walks through it step by step.

  • Choose the most specific primary category (for example "Plumber" rather than "Contractor").
  • Add additional categories only for things you really do.
  • Upload real photos of your work, team, vehicles and premises, and keep adding new ones.
  • Post updates and specials from time to time, so the profile looks active.

2. Get reviews, and reply to them

Reviews affect both your ranking and whether people choose you. Ask every happy customer, ideally soon after the job, with a direct review link. Reply to every review, good or bad, politely and professionally.

Do not buy reviews, offer discounts in exchange for them, or ask only happy customers to review you while discouraging others. Google's rules forbid this, and fake or incentivised reviews can be removed or get the profile suspended.

3. Keep your name, address and phone consistent

Your business name, address and phone number (often called NAP) should be identical everywhere: your website, Google profile, Facebook page, WhatsApp Business profile and directories. Small differences, such as an old number or "St" on one site and "Street" on another, are normal, but an outdated phone number or old address confuses customers and Google.

Listing your business on reputable South African directories such as Yep!, Brabys and industry associations adds more consistent mentions of your business.

4. Give each main service its own page

A single "Services" page with a list of ten bullet points gives Google little to work with. A page for each main service, with a clear title like "Geyser installation in Johannesburg", explains what you do, the areas you cover, typical questions and prices, and how to book. That is far more likely to appear when someone searches for that service.

  • Write for customers first: what the service includes, how long it takes, what it costs or what affects the price.
  • Mention the areas you serve naturally, without repeating them unnaturally.
  • Add real photos of that work.

5. Get the basics right on your website

  • Page titles: each page should have a unique title that says what it is and where, like "Laptop Repairs in Pretoria | Business Name".
  • Mobile-friendly: most local searches happen on phones. Text must be readable and buttons easy to tap.
  • Fast: compress large photos and avoid heavy page builders where possible.
  • Click-to-call and WhatsApp buttons on every page.
  • Your address and service area in the footer or contact page.
  • HTTPS (the padlock) on every page.

6. Add Google Search Console

Google Search Console is a free tool that shows which searches your website appears for, how many people click, and any problems Google finds. Add your site, submit your sitemap, and check it once a month. It tells you which pages are working and which need improving.

7. Earn local mentions and links

Links from other relevant websites tell Google your business is real and trusted. Good sources are suppliers you work with, local business associations, community events you sponsor, and clients who are happy to mention you. Avoid paying for bulk links, which can do more harm than good.

8. Be patient and keep it going

Local SEO builds up over weeks and months, not days. A complete profile, a steady flow of genuine reviews, and useful pages for each service will usually move you up over time, especially against competitors who have not done the basics.

Common questions

Can I rank in areas where I do not have an office?

For the map results, distance matters, so it is harder to appear far from your address. Setting a service area on your Google profile and writing helpful pages about the areas you genuinely serve helps your website appear in the normal results for those areas.

Should I pay for Google Ads instead?

Ads put you at the top immediately, which is useful while your free (organic) rankings build. Many businesses use both: ads for quick results, and local SEO for lasting, free visibility.

How long does it take to see results?

Fixing your Google profile can show results within a few weeks. Website changes and reviews usually take a few months to have their full effect, depending on how competitive your area and industry are.
