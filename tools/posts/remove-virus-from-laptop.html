When people tell us their laptop has a virus, the cause is usually one of three things: adware that came bundled with a free download, a fake "PC cleaner" that shows scary warnings, or a website that was allowed to send browser notifications. All three can be removed without wiping the laptop. Work through these steps in order.

First: if files are locked, stop here

If your documents have been renamed with strange extensions, will not open, and there is a message asking for payment, that is ransomware. Do not pay and do not keep using the laptop. Unplug the network cable or switch off Wi-Fi, shut it down, and get professional help. Running cleaning tools at this point can make recovery harder.

For everything else, carry on.

1. Stop the browser notification pop-ups

Pop-ups in the corner of the screen saying "Your PC is infected" are very often website notifications, not a virus. At some point a site asked to "show notifications" and someone clicked Allow.

  • Chrome: Settings → Privacy and security → Site settings → Notifications. Remove any site you do not recognise from the Allowed list.
  • Edge: Settings → Cookies and site permissions → Notifications. Remove the same way.

If the pop-ups stop, that was the whole problem.

2. Remove programs you did not install

Open Settings → Apps → Installed apps and sort by install date. Look for anything installed around the time the problems started, especially "optimizers", "cleaners", "speed boosters", unknown browsers, or toolbars. Uninstall them.

If you are not sure what a program is, search its exact name online before removing it. Leave anything from your laptop's manufacturer (HP, Dell, Lenovo, Acer, ASUS) and drivers for sound, graphics or the touchpad.

3. Clean up your browser

Adware often changes your homepage and search engine and adds extensions.

  • Open your browser's Extensions page and remove anything you do not use or recognise.
  • In Chrome, go to Settings → Reset settings → Restore settings to their original defaults. In Edge, use Settings → Reset settings. This keeps your bookmarks and saved passwords.
  • Check that your search engine is set back to Google or Bing.

4. Run a full scan, then an offline scan

Windows already includes good antivirus. Open Windows Security → Virus & threat protection → Scan options:

  1. Run a Full scan and remove anything it finds. It can take an hour or more.
  2. Then run a Microsoft Defender Offline scan from the same screen. The laptop restarts and scans before Windows loads, which catches threats that hide while Windows is running.

You do not need to buy an extra antivirus to do this. Having two antivirus programs installed at the same time can actually slow the laptop down and cause conflicts.

5. Change your passwords

If the laptop was infected, assume passwords typed on it may have been seen. After it is clean, change your email password first (it is used to reset everything else), then banking, social media and any work accounts. Turn on two-step verification wherever it is offered.

6. Make it less likely to happen again

  • Download software only from the official website or the Microsoft Store, not from download sites that wrap installers in extra offers.
  • During any installation, read each screen and untick "also install" offers.
  • Click Block when a site you do not know asks to show notifications.
  • Keep Windows Update on, and restart when it asks.
  • Keep a backup of important files on an external drive or in the cloud, so ransomware cannot take everything.

When to get help

Ask a technician if scans keep finding the same threat after removal, if Windows Security will not open or switches itself off, if the laptop is so slow you cannot complete a scan, or if business data or banking may have been exposed. In those cases a proper clean-up, or a backup and fresh install of Windows, is safer than trying more tools.
