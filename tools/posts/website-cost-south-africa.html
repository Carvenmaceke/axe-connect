Ask three people to quote for "a website" and you can get three very different numbers. That is not always because someone is overcharging. "A website" can mean a single page with your contact details, or a full system that takes bookings and payments. Knowing what goes into the price helps you compare quotes properly and avoid paying for things you do not need.

The running costs every website has

Whoever builds your site, you will pay for these every year or every month:

  • Domain name. Your address, such as yourbusiness.co.za. It is renewed every year. A .co.za domain is usually much cheaper than .com.
  • Hosting. The server your website lives on, usually paid monthly or yearly. It often includes business email addresses on your domain.
  • SSL certificate. The padlock in the browser. Most good South African hosts include a free one, so you should not be paying extra for it.
Ask who the domain will be registered to. It should be registered in your name or your business's name, even if your developer manages it for you. Otherwise moving away later can be difficult.

What changes the once-off build price

How many pages

A one-page site with your services, photos and contact details is the simplest. Separate pages for each service, an about page, a gallery and a contact page take more design and writing time.

Template or custom design

A lightly adjusted template is quicker and cheaper. A design built around your brand, colours and customers costs more but helps you stand out from competitors using the same template.

Features

This is where prices climb the most. Each of these adds work:

  • Online bookings with a calendar and available time slots
  • Online payments through a South African gateway such as PayFast or Yoco
  • An online shop with products, stock and orders
  • Customer accounts and logins
  • An admin dashboard for you to manage bookings, orders or clients

Content

If you supply the wording, logo and photos, the build is faster. If the developer needs to write the text, design a logo or source images, expect that to be quoted separately.

Maintenance after launch

Ask whether the quote includes updates after the site is live. Some developers charge per change; others offer a monthly plan that covers small updates, backups and security checks. A monthly plan usually makes sense if your prices, menu or specials change often.

Website builder or developer?

Do-it-yourself builders like Wix or Squarespace have low starting costs and work well if you have time to set them up and keep them updated yourself. The trade-offs are monthly fees that add up, limits on what you can customise, and the time it takes you. A developer costs more upfront but builds what your business needs and handles the technical side, which frees you to run the business.

How to compare quotes

When quotes differ a lot, ask each one the same questions:

  1. Is the domain and hosting included, and for how long?
  2. Who owns the domain and the website files?
  3. How many pages and which features are included?
  4. Will it work properly on phones?
  5. Is basic SEO (page titles, descriptions, Google Search Console) included?
  6. How many rounds of changes are included before launch?
  7. What does support cost after launch?

The cheapest quote is not always the best value, and the most expensive is not always the best site. A clear quote that answers all seven questions is a good sign either way.
