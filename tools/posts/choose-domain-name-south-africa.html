Your domain name ends up everywhere: on your website, your email address, your business cards, your vehicle signage and every invoice you send. Changing it later means reprinting, re-telling customers and losing some of the Google ranking you built up. A few minutes of thought before you register saves a lot of trouble.

.co.za, .com or something else?

For most businesses that trade mainly in South Africa, .co.za is the best choice:

  • It tells people you are local. A customer in Pretoria who sees yourbusiness.co.za knows straight away they are dealing with a South African business.
  • It is usually cheaper to register and renew than .com, and short, sensible names are far more likely to still be available.
  • Google uses it as a signal that your site is relevant to people searching in South Africa.

.com makes sense when you sell internationally, or when your brand is not tied to one country. Some businesses register both and point the .com at the .co.za site, so nobody else can take it.

Newer endings such as .africa, .online or .shop are fine for a side project or campaign, but customers still type .co.za and .com out of habit. For your main business address, stick with what people expect.

Six rules for a good domain name

1. Make it easy to say out loud

Imagine telling someone your email address over the phone. If you have to spell it, explain a hyphen or say "the number 4, not the word", it will cost you emails and enquiries. mokoenaplumbing.co.za is easy. mokoena-plumbing-4u.co.za is not.

2. Keep it short

Aim for something you can type on a phone without mistakes. If your full trading name is long, use the part people actually call you, or a shortened version.

3. Avoid hyphens and numbers

People forget hyphens and mix up "2" and "two". If the name you want is taken, a different word usually works better than adding a hyphen.

4. Match your business name

Your domain should look like the name on your signage. Customers trust it more, and it makes your Google Business Profile, website and social pages feel like one business.

5. Think about what you might sell later

A domain like pretorialaptoprepairs.co.za describes one service in one city. If you later add networks or office support, or open in Johannesburg, the name starts working against you. Your business name is usually the safer choice.

6. Check it is not someone else's brand

Do not register a name that copies another company's trademark, even if the domain is available. Search the name on Google and on the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) website first. A trademark dispute can force you to give the domain up after you have printed it everywhere.

Register it in your own name

This is the most important point in this guide. The domain must be registered to you or your business, with your details as the registrant, even if a developer or IT company sets it up for you.

We regularly meet business owners whose domain was registered by a former web designer, a relative or a staff member who has since left. When that person is unreachable, the business cannot move its website, change its email provider or even renew the domain without a long dispute. Ask for proof of who the registrant is, and keep the login details for the account where the domain is managed.

Plan for renewals

A domain is rented, not bought. You pay to register it, then renew it every year (or every few years if you pay upfront). If a renewal is missed, your website and email stop working, and after a grace period someone else can register the name.

  • Make sure renewal reminders go to an email address that someone actually reads.
  • Switch on auto-renew if your registrar offers it, and keep the card on file up to date.
  • Put the renewal date in your business calendar as a backup.

Use it for email straight away

Once you own a domain, use it for email. info@yourbusiness.co.za looks far more established than a free Gmail address, and it keeps working if you change staff. Most hosting packages include business email, or you can connect the domain to Google Workspace or Microsoft 365. Our guide on setting up business email on your phone walks through the settings.

A quick checklist before you pay

  1. Can you say it over the phone without spelling it?
  2. Is it short, without hyphens or numbers?
  3. Does it match the name on your signage?
  4. Will it still fit if your services grow?
  5. Have you checked it is not another company's brand?
  6. Will it be registered in your name, with renewal reminders going to you?

Common questions

Can I change my domain name later?

Yes, but it takes work. You register the new domain, move the website and email across, and set up redirects from the old address so links and Google rankings carry over. Keep the old domain renewed for at least a year so emails sent to the old address still arrive.

Should I buy several versions of my name?

If your brand matters, registering the .com as well as the .co.za is worth it, and so is a common misspelling if customers often get it wrong. Point the extra domains at your main website. There is no need to buy every ending that exists.

Does the domain name affect Google rankings?

A little. A .co.za helps Google understand that you serve South Africa. Stuffing keywords into the name (like best-cheap-plumber-pretoria.co.za) does not help much anymore and makes you look less trustworthy. Good content, reviews and a complete Google Business Profile matter far more.
