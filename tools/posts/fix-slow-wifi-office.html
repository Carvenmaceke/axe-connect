"The internet is slow" is one of the most common calls we get. Very often the fibre or LTE line is fine, and the real problem is the Wi-Fi between the router and your device. These checks help you find out which it is, and fix most problems without spending money.

1. Find out if it is the Wi-Fi or the line

Plug a laptop directly into the router with a network cable and run a speed test (for example on speedtest.net or fast.com). Then run the same test on Wi-Fi, standing next to the router, and again from the room where the problem is.

  • Slow even on the cable: the problem is the line or your internet provider. Restart the fibre box and router, then contact your provider with your test results.
  • Fast on cable, slow on Wi-Fi far away: it is a Wi-Fi coverage problem. Keep reading.

2. Restart the router and fibre box properly

Switch off the router and the fibre box (ONT) at the wall, wait 30 seconds, switch the fibre box on first and wait for its lights to settle, then switch on the router. It clears up many random slowdowns and dropped connections, especially after power cuts.

3. Move the router

Wi-Fi signals weaken with distance and struggle through walls, especially brick and concrete, which are common in South African buildings. Routers hidden in a cupboard, behind a TV or on the floor in a corner perform badly.

  • Place it high up, in the open, and as central as possible to where people work.
  • Keep it away from microwaves, cordless phones, fish tanks and large metal objects.
  • If the fibre point is in a bad spot, a longer network cable to a better position can make a big difference.

4. Use the right band: 2.4 GHz or 5 GHz

Most routers broadcast two bands:

  • 5 GHz: much faster, but shorter range. Best for laptops and phones in the same room or the next one.
  • 2.4 GHz: slower, but reaches further and through more walls. Fine for printers and smart devices.

If your router shows two network names (for example "Office" and "Office_5G"), connect nearby devices to the 5 GHz network. Many newer routers use one name and choose the band automatically.

5. Change the Wi-Fi channel

In complexes, office parks and townhouses, dozens of neighbouring networks compete on the same channels. Log in to your router (the address and password are usually on a sticker underneath) and set the channel to automatic, or try a less crowded channel. On 2.4 GHz, channels 1, 6 and 11 are the ones that do not overlap.

6. Update the router

Router manufacturers release firmware updates that fix bugs, improve speed and close security holes. Check the router's admin page for an update option. If the router is many years old and does not support the newer Wi-Fi 5 or Wi-Fi 6 standards, a replacement may be the real fix.

7. Too many devices?

Every phone, laptop, TV, camera and smart plug shares the same Wi-Fi. A cheap router can struggle with a busy office or household. Check the list of connected devices in the router's admin page, remove anything you do not recognise, and change the Wi-Fi password if strangers are connected.

Set up a separate guest network for visitors and customers. It keeps them off your business network and protects your shared files and printers.

8. Extenders, mesh or access points

If one router cannot cover the whole space, you need more Wi-Fi points:

  • Wi-Fi extenders are cheap, but often halve the speed and create a separate network name. Fine for one far room.
  • Mesh systems use two or three units that work together as one network, so devices move between them smoothly. Good for houses and small offices.
  • Wired access points connected back to the router by network cable give the best speed and reliability, and are the right choice for offices with several rooms or floors.

9. Check the device itself

If only one laptop is slow while others are fine, the problem is that laptop. Update its Wi-Fi drivers and operating system, "forget" the network and reconnect, and check for programs using a lot of internet in the background, such as cloud backups or updates. Our guide on fixing a slow laptop covers more.

Keep the internet on during power cuts

A small UPS (uninterruptible power supply) or a DC router UPS can keep your fibre box and router running for several hours during power cuts, so laptops on battery stay connected. Check that the UPS has the right output for your devices.

Common questions

Will a faster fibre package fix my Wi-Fi?

Only if the line itself is the bottleneck. If the speed is good on a cable but poor on Wi-Fi in other rooms, a faster package will not help. Better router placement or mesh will.

Why does my Wi-Fi drop every evening?

Often it is congestion: neighbours' networks are busiest in the evening. Switching to 5 GHz or changing channels usually helps. If the connection drops on a cable too, contact your internet provider.

Is it safe to use the default Wi-Fi password?

Change the default router admin password and use a strong Wi-Fi password with WPA2 or WPA3 security. Default admin passwords are widely known and are an easy way in for attackers.
